02 — The Workshop

Six people, one bench, no assembly line

Kestrel Modular started in a garage in 2014, when our founder Dana Whitlock got tired of synthesizers that asked her to update firmware before she could make a sound. Today the shop is still small on purpose: six builders, one calibration bench, and a waitlist we're honest about instead of hiding.

Point-to-point wiring

Every unit is hand-wired and hand-calibrated against a reference oscillator before it ships. No two boards leave the shop with identical tuning curves — that's the analog part.

Repair-for-life policy

If a Kestrel instrument breaks in twenty years, mail it back. We keep schematics and spare panel stock for every model we've ever made.

03 — Why Analog

Voltage doesn't have a preset button

We're not anti-digital — half the shop uses digital multitrack recorders. But a synthesizer is different: it's an instrument you learn with your hands, the same way you learn where the frets are on a guitar. Presets teach you to recall someone else's sound. Knobs teach you to find your own.

Temperature drift, on purpose

Our oscillators drift slightly as they warm up, the way vintage originals do. Some customers ask us to "fix" this. We don't — it's part of why analog still sounds like analog.

Serviceable for decades

Through-hole components and socketed ICs only. A tech in 2050 should be able to open the case and understand exactly what they're looking at.

04 — Visit & Custom Builds

Come play before you buy

Every instrument on the floor is patched in and ready to go — we'd rather you spend twenty minutes with a synth than read another spec sheet. We also take on a small number of custom builds each year for players who need something we don't make yet.
