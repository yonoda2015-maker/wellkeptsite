The Last Projectionist of Yesler Way

For forty-one years, Marion Okabe has threaded 35mm film through the same Century JJ projector. The neighborhood changed around her booth. The booth did not.

By Dana Reyes · Photographs by T. Adeyemi · 18 min read

The booth of the Corona Theater, Seattle, photographed in March. The carbon-arc housings were retired in 1988; Okabe kept one as a doorstop.

The staircase to the booth is seventeen steps, and Marion Okabe has counted them every working night since 1985. She climbs them with a thermos, a paperback, and two reels of whatever the Corona is showing — lately a restored print of a Taiwanese drama that arrived in a dented orange shipping case, the kind she says smells like every projection room she has ever stood in.

Downstairs, the theater has survived three owners, one fire, and a decade of streaming. Upstairs, almost nothing has changed. There is a reason for that, and it is not nostalgia.
